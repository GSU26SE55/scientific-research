\begin{abstract}
Solar energy and electric vehicle growth depend on lithium-ion battery
monitoring. Capacity decrease may cause considerable economic losses and
safety problems if ignored. State of Health (SOH) is a key battery health
indicator, but real-time estimation from raw sensor data is computationally
and methodologically difficult. Transformer designs are too expensive for
edge situations, and traditional recurrent models struggle to learn
long-term temporal connections in degradation cycles. We provide
MambaSOHPredictor, a lightweight Selective State Space Model (Mamba)
framework with physics-informed spectral feature conditioning. The
architecture has a compact 30-step window variant for real-time edge
inference and a high-accuracy long-sequence model ($L = 4096$) for full
discharge cycles. With linear $O(L)$ complexity, MambaBlocks processes
multivariate time-series data (voltage, current, temperature). A
57-dimensional cycle-level spectral and statistical feature vector is used
for FiLM. Uncertainty estimation employs Monte Carlo Dropout, while anomaly
detection uses Isolation Forest simultaneously in the same feature space. To
prioritise predictive maintenance, the results are combined into a risk
matrix. The long-sequence model obtains an MAE of 1.52\% and an RMSE of
1.97\% on the NASA Ames dataset under a strict cross-battery approach, exceeding
industry norms. This modest setup can infer in under 100\,ms on CPU,
enabling real-time edge deployment without GPU acceleration.

\end{abstract}
